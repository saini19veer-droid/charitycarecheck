Hospital charity care in Washington

Washington's charity care law requires hospitals to provide free care to eligible low-income patients and discounted care above that, with some of the broadest rules in the country.

Income thresholdsKey protectionsFind your policy
Free care

At or below 200% FPL

Washington hospitals must provide free care to patients at or below 200% of the federal poverty level.

Discounted care

Above 200% FPL

Discounts continue above 200% FPL; at least 300% FPL, and higher for some hospitals.

Applies to

Hospitals statewide

Washington's charity care law covers hospitals licensed in the state, not just nonprofits.

Federal floor

501(r)

Every nonprofit hospital must have a written financial assistance policy, limit charges to patients who qualify to no more than amounts generally billed to insured patients, and wait before extraordinary collection actions (IRS section 501(r)).

Income thresholds

Washington charity care income thresholds

Income levelWhat the rules provide
At or below 200% FPLFree care
200%–300% FPLDiscounted care (tiers vary by hospital size and policy)
Above 300% FPLDepends on the hospital; larger hospitals may extend discounts further

For 2026, 200% FPL for a family of four is $66,000. Washington law has been amended recently, so verify current tier levels with the Department of Health or the hospital.

Key protections

Protections that apply in Washington

  • Charity care law

    State law requires hospitals to adopt charity care policies and to screen patients for eligibility.

    State law

  • Collection limits

    Washington restricts collection against patients who qualify. Ask for collections to pause while your application is pending.

    Limits

  • Federal 501(r)

    Nonprofit hospitals must also meet federal collection timing and charge limits.

    Federal right

Apply even if you have insurance

Washington's charity care can apply to remaining balances after insurance in some situations. Ask the hospital whether your balance qualifies.

How to look up a specific hospital's rules

Search the hospital name plus "charity care". The Washington Department of Health publishes charity care information and takes complaints.
