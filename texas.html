State guide

Hospital charity care in Texas

Texas requires nonprofit hospitals to maintain charity care policies and report community benefits, but it does not set one statewide income cutoff. Your eligibility depends largely on the hospital's own published policy.

Income thresholdsKey protectionsFind your policy
State law

Chapter 311

Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 311 covers hospital charity care and community benefits reporting.

Income cutoff

Set by hospital

Texas does not set a single statewide income threshold; each hospital sets its own eligibility levels.

Applies to

Nonprofit hospitals

Charity care requirements are tied to nonprofit hospital status, so for-profit hospitals have fewer obligations.

Federal floor

501(r)

Every nonprofit hospital must have a written financial assistance policy, limit charges to patients who qualify to no more than amounts generally billed to insured patients, and wait before extraordinary collection actions (IRS section 501(r)).

Income thresholds

Texas charity care income thresholds

Income levelWhat the rules provide
Any incomeNo statewide cutoff; read the hospital's policy
Federal ruleNonprofits must limit charges for eligible patients to amounts generally billed to insured patients
For-profit hospitalsNo state charity mandate; ask for a self-pay discount or payment plan

Many large Texas systems extend free or discounted care to roughly 200%–300% FPL or more, but this varies; treat any figure as hospital-specific and confirm it in writing.

Key protections

Protections that apply in Texas

  • Charity care policy

    Nonprofit hospitals must adopt and follow a charity care policy and report on community benefits.

    State law

  • Federal 501(r)

    Written policy, limit on charges, and collection waiting periods for nonprofits.

    Federal right

  • Emergency care

    Hospitals with emergency departments must screen and stabilize you regardless of ability to pay (EMTALA).

    Federal right

Check Medicaid and CHIP

Texas has not expanded Medicaid to most low-income adults, so many adults will not qualify, but children and pregnant people often do. Check with Texas Health and Human Services.

Find your hospital's policy

How to look up a specific hospital's rules

Search the hospital name plus "financial assistance policy" or "charity care application". Ask the billing office for a paper copy if you cannot find one online.
