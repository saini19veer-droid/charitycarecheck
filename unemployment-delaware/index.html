How Delaware Unemployment Benefits Are Calculated

Base period: first 4 of last 5 completed quarters. Benefit = 1/46 of total wages across your two highest quarters, capped at $450 — raised from $400 by House Bill 49 in March 2024, the first increase since 2019. Delaware pays no dependent allowance and requires no waiting week.

Delaware uses total wages from your two highest quarters rather than a single high quarter to calculate your benefit.

Eligibility Requirements in Delaware

To qualify for unemployment benefits in Delaware, you must meet all of these conditions:

  • Unemployed through no fault of your own — laid off, position eliminated, or company closed. Voluntary resignation or termination for cause generally disqualifies you.
  • Earned sufficient wages during the base period — Delaware looks at your earnings in the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters before you filed.
  • Able and available to work — you must be actively seeking employment and available to accept suitable work if offered.
  • Registered with Delaware workforce services — most states require you to register with their job search system as a condition of receiving benefits.
File immediately — every day costs you money Delaware has no waiting period, but your claim only starts from the date you file — delaying still costs you real days. File at ui.delawareworks.com the same day you are laid off, or the next morning at the latest.

How Much You Can Earn While Still Collecting in Delaware

You can earn up to the greater of $10 or 50% of your weekly benefit amount without a reduction.

How to File for Unemployment in Delaware

  • File online at ui.delawareworks.com — this is the fastest method and available 24/7.
  • Log at least 1 job contact per week in a work-search log — the Division of Unemployment Insurance can audit this at any time.
  • Have ready: your Social Security number, employer name and address, start and end dates of employment, and reason for separation.
  • Report your severance — if you received severance pay, you must report it. Delaware may treat it as wages which could delay the start of your benefits.
  • Certify weekly — after filing, you must certify your job search activity every week to continue receiving payments. Missing a certification week can pause your benefits.
  • Report any income — if you earn money through freelancing, part-time work, or gig work while collecting benefits, you must report it. Most states reduce your benefit by the amount earned over a small threshold.
Taxes on unemployment benefits Unemployment benefits are taxable as ordinary income at the federal level. You can request 10% federal withholding when you file — highly recommended. If you don't withhold, you will owe the taxes when you file your return, which can be a painful surprise when you are already financially stretched.

What's Different About Filing in Delaware

A few things are distinctly Delaware:

  • No waiting week — a real, if less commonly known, exception to the norm most states follow.
  • The cap only recently rose after a 5-year freeze. House Bill 49 raised the maximum from $400 to $450 in March 2024, the first increase since 2019, specifically to keep pace with cost-of-living increases.
  • The formula uses two quarters combined, divided by 46 — a distinctive divisor most states don't use.
  • No dependent allowance at all — your benefit is based solely on wage history.
  • Extended Benefits exist but are currently inactive — Delaware's EB program can add up to 13 weeks if the state's unemployment rate triggers it, but it wasn't active as of early 2026.

How Delaware Compares to Other States

Delaware's $450 maximum sits below most Mid-Atlantic neighbors — Pennsylvania pays up to $605, New Jersey up to $905 — though Delaware's lack of a waiting week is a genuine advantage those states don't offer.
