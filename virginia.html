Hospital charity care in Virginia

In Virginia, the strongest baseline protections come from federal law for nonprofit hospitals. Eligibility levels are mostly set by each hospital's own financial assistance policy, so read it before assuming you do or don't qualify.

Income thresholdsKey protectionsFind your policy
Federal floor

501(r)

Every nonprofit hospital must have a written financial assistance policy, limit charges to patients who qualify to no more than amounts generally billed to insured patients, and wait before extraordinary collection actions (IRS section 501(r)).

Eligibility levels

Set by hospital

We have not confirmed a single statewide income cutoff for Virginia; confirm the specific hospital's tiers.

Medicaid

Expanded

Virginia expanded Medicaid in 2019, so many adults with low incomes can get coverage instead of charity care.

Emergency care

EMTALA

Emergency departments must screen and stabilize you regardless of ability to pay.

Income thresholds

Virginia charity care income thresholds

Income levelWhat the rules provide
Low incomeOften free care under the hospital's policy; the cutoff varies
Moderate incomeSliding-scale discounts at many nonprofit hospitals
Any incomeNonprofits must cap charges for eligible patients; everyone can ask for a self-pay discount or payment plan

Because tiers differ by hospital, collect the written policy and compare your household income to its table (200% FPL for a family of four is $66,000 in 2026).

Key protections

Protections that apply in Virginia

  • Federal 501(r) limits

    Nonprofit hospitals wait at least 120 days after the first bill before extraordinary collection actions and take applications for 240 days.

    Federal right

  • Medicaid first

    Apply for Medicaid if you may qualify; coverage can sometimes reach back to recent bills.

    Check first

  • Written policy

    Nonprofits must publish their policy and a plain-language summary.

    Published

Verify state specifics

State rules change. Confirm current Virginia requirements with the Virginia Department of Health or Office of the Attorney General before relying on any state-level protection.
